Karma: WordGuesserAI (py_evil_hangman/word_guesser.py), run with -k

Design
- __init__ reads the dictionary once and buckets words by length.
- get_guess() is a pure "reflex" agent: each turn it rebuilds the candidate set only from the
  visible GameState. A word stays a candidate if every revealed position matches, and no hidden
  position holds a letter that was already guessed (it would have been revealed there).
- For each unguessed letter it partitions the candidates into position-families (the same way the
  evil word-maker does) and scores the letter by its worst-case (largest) family. It picks the
  letter with the smallest worst case, i.e. the letter whose answer is guaranteed to cut the word
  space the most, even against an adversary. Ties prefer letters whose worst case is a hit rather
  than a miss (a miss costs a guess), then letters that appear in more candidates.
- Letters that appear in no candidate are skipped (a guaranteed miss). If no candidates remain
  (the word isn't in the guesser's dictionary) it falls back to English letter-frequency order.

Assumptions
- The guesser and word-maker share the same dictionary.
- The word-maker may be adversarial, so minimax is used instead of maximising expected information.
  Against the honest WordMakerHuman this is still a strong strategy.

Results (10 guesses, default dictionary): the evil maker usually wins on short words (2-5 letters,
e.g. "buck", "cuddy") since the dictionary has so many near-identical short words; the AI generally
wins on 8+ letter words (e.g. solved an 8-letter word with 5 misses, a 12-letter word with 3).
